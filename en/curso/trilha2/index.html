🧠 Become irreplaceable

AI performs more and more tasks. What it doesn’t replace are the 12 human skills—and the WEF (Future of Jobs 2025) confirms these are exactly the skills companies value most. This learning path develops these skills, divided into mind (module 2.1) and relationships and character (module 2.2).

2.1 ~90 min · 6 topics

🧠 Thinking skills

Critical thinking, creativity, nexialism, breadth of knowledge, data literacy, and prioritization. The cognitive skills that determine what AI does — and distinguish those who use AI with judgment from those who become dependent on it.

What is:

Question information, separate fact from opinion, and make decisions based on evidence—not on what sounds convincing.

Why learn:

AI invents data with confidence (hallucination). Without critical thinking, you become hostage to the machine’s answer.

Key concepts:

Evidence on eloquence · hallucination · WEF’s #1 skill (analytical thinking, 69%).

What is:

Combine references, imagine possibilities, and find different solutions—drawing on experience, emotion, and purpose.

Why learn:

AI recombines what already exists; the spark of a new problem and the taste that decides what’s worthwhile are still human.

Key concepts:

Recombination vs. origination · diverge and converge · WEF #4 (creative thinking, 57%).

What is:

See connections between technology, behavior, economics, and culture — connect dots that seem unrelated.

Why learn:

AI delivers specific answers; the human with a broad perspective connects the dots using tacit knowledge.

Key concepts:

Tacit knowledge · interdisciplinarity · domain judgment (Princeton/Mitchell).

What is:

The body of experiences, reading, and examples that supports good questions and sound judgment.

Why learn:

People with limited knowledge depend too much on others—including AI—and don't know how to evaluate its answer.

Key concepts:

A knowledge base fuels the prompt · curate sources · read beyond your own field.

What is:

Reading, interpreting, and questioning data to make better decisions—without being fooled by a pretty chart or vanity metric.

Why learn:

The #1 evaluation failure is trusting the vendor’s metric instead of questioning the “ground truth” (the actual answer key).

Key concepts:

Ground truth · vanity metric · WEF #6 (technological literacy, 51%).

What is:

Decide which problem matters and what should be done — not just how to do it faster.

Why learn:

Execution got cheap; making the right decisions became more valuable. The METR illusion shows that perceived speed can be misleading.

Key concepts:

Execution ≠ decision-making · the METR illusion · opportunity cost.

2.2 ~90 min · 6 topics

🤝 Relationship and character skills

Empathy, human connection, drive and purpose, dealing with uncertainty, learning how to learn, and ethical courage. What machines don’t feel — and what becomes more valuable as the world grows more digital.

What is:

Put yourself deeply in someone else’s shoes—their pain, fears, and context—and act accordingly.

Why learn:

AI simulates empathy in words, but it doesn’t feel or form real bonds. Genuine connection is a human advantage.

Key concepts:

Simulated vs. felt empathy · active listening · WEF #7 (empathy and active listening, 50%).

What is:

Building trust, collaboration, and real relationships — leading and influencing people.

Why learn:

The more digital the world, the more valuable genuine connection becomes. People trust and buy from people.

Key concepts:

Trust · presence · WEF #3 (leadership and social influence, 61%).

What is:

Will is the energy to act; purpose is clarity about why. Together, they provide direction.

Why learn:

AI has no desires of its own—it doesn’t want to build a life, fulfill a dream, or stand up for a cause. You do.

Key concepts:

Intrinsic motivation · self-awareness · WEF #5 (motivation and self-awareness, 52%).

What is:

Act, adapt, and keep going even without complete certainty—resilience in the face of change.

Why learn:

The world changes fast, and AI speeds things up even more. Those who need absolute certainty before acting become paralyzed.

Key concepts:

Resilience · small bets · WEF #2 (resilience, flexibility, and agility, 67%).

What is:

Keep learning, let go of what’s outdated, and relearn what has changed.

Why learn:

With AI, technical knowledge becomes outdated quickly. ~39% of skills will change by 2030 (WEF).

Key concepts:

Unlearn · AI’s learning gap (MIT) · WEF #8 (curiosity and lifelong learning, 50%).

What is:

Have principles and act on them in a world where technology can be used both to help and to manipulate.

Why learn:

The key question is no longer just “is it possible?” but “is it possible, and is it right?” Don’t outsource that to the machine.

Key concepts:

“It’s possible, but is it right?” · responsibility · don’t outsource the decision to AI.
